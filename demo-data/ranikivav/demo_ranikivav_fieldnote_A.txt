Monument: Rani ki Vav (Queen's Stepwell), Patan, Gujarat
Recorded by: Team KathaGraph, note A

Rani ki Vav stands on the banks of the old Saraswati river at Patan, the former capital of the Chaulukya (Solanki) kings.
The stepwell was built by Queen Udayamati in memory of her husband, King Bhima I, and was completed around 1063 CE.
The source usually cited for Udayamati is the Prabandha Chintamani, written by Merutunga in 1304.

The stepwell is about 64 m long, 20 m wide and 27 m deep, and descends in seven levels to the well shaft.
Walls and pillars carry more than 500 principal sculptures, most of them showing the avatars of Vishnu.
Material: sandstone.

The river flooded the site and silted it up for centuries. It was excavated and restored by the Archaeological Survey of India in the 1980s.
It was inscribed as a UNESCO World Heritage Site in 2014.
